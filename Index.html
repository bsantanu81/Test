The DPDP Act, 2023:
Protecting the people behind the data

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act changes how we collect, use, store and share personal data. Every phone number, PAN card, Aadhaar copy and site-visit form belongs to a real person who trusted us. This guide covers what the law asks of you, what to avoid, and why it matters.

The basics

What is the DPDP Act?

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is India's first full law on digital personal data. It covers personal data collected digitally, and also paper data that is later digitised. It applies to any organisation that processes such data in India, and to processing outside India when it is linked to offering goods or services to people in India.

The core idea in one line

Collect only what you need, for a purpose you have clearly told the person about, with their consent. Keep it safe, use it only for that purpose, and delete it when the purpose is over.

Who does it protect? Customers, leads, channel partners, vendors, job applicants and employees. Anyone whose personal data we hold.

Day-to-day behaviour

What we should do, and what we shouldn't

Practical rules for sales, CRM, site visits, bookings, marketing and back office.

✔

Take clear consent before collecting data

Tell the person what you are collecting and why, and get an explicit "yes". Use the approved consent form or checkbox, never a pre-ticked one.

💡 Site-visit forms should say: "We'll use your details to share project information and follow up on your enquiry."
✔

Collect only what is needed

Data minimisation. An enquiry needs a name and phone number, not PAN, Aadhaar or income details. Ask for KYC documents only at the booking stage.

✔

Store data only in approved systems

Use the official CRM or company drive. Personal data belongs in systems with access control and audit logs.

💡 Received an Aadhaar or PAN copy on WhatsApp? Upload it to the CRM, then delete it from your phone.
✔

Use data only for the stated purpose

If a customer shared details for Project A, check their consent before marketing other projects or passing them to partners.

✔

Respect "stop" and "delete" requests right away

When someone withdraws consent or asks for erasure, log it and pass it to the Data Protection / Grievance Officer. Stop all calls and messages.

✔

Report any suspected breach immediately

Lost laptop, wrong email recipient, phishing click, leaked sheet: report it to IT or the DPO the same hour. The company has to notify the Board and affected people without delay.

✔

Lock devices and use strong passwords

Screen lock, 2-factor authentication, no shared logins. Log out of the CRM on shared computers.

✔

Handle children's data with extra care

Anyone under 18 is a child. Get verifiable parental consent, and never track or target children with ads.

✘

Don't buy or use purchased lead lists

"HNI databases" and scraped lists come without valid consent. Calling these numbers puts the company directly at risk.

✘

Don't share customer data on personal WhatsApp groups

Forwarding KYC documents, phone numbers or payment details to personal chats or brokers outside approved channels is a disclosure risk.

✘

Don't download data to personal devices or email

No exporting CRM data to Excel on a personal laptop, pen drive or Gmail. If it isn't in a secured system, we can't protect it.

✘

Don't take customer data with you when you leave

Lead lists and contacts belong to the data principal and the company, not to you. Taking them is a breach and can also be a criminal offence.

✘

Don't keep data forever "just in case"

When the purpose is over and there's no legal need to keep the data, it must be erased under the retention policy.

✘

Don't bundle or force consent

"Agree to marketing calls or you can't book a site visit" is not valid consent. Consent must be free and unconditional.

✘

Don't leave documents lying around

Printed booking forms, cheque copies and KYC files on desks or in site-office drawers are breaches waiting to happen. Lock them away or shred them.

✘

Don't hide or delay a breach

Covering up a mistake turns a manageable incident into a serious violation. Failing to notify can cost up to ₹200 crore.

What customers can ask of us

Rights of the Data Principal

Every customer and lead has these rights. If someone asks, don't brush them off. Route the request to the designated officer.

📄 Right to information

To know what personal data we hold about them, how it is being processed, and who we have shared it with.

✏️ Right to correction & erasure

To correct inaccurate data, complete or update it, and have it erased when it's no longer needed (unless the law requires us to keep it).

↩️ Right to withdraw consent

At any time, and as easily as it was given. After withdrawal, we must stop processing, and so must our vendors.

🗣️ Right to grievance redressal

To raise a complaint with us and get a response within the prescribed time (up to 90 days under the Rules). If unhappy, they can escalate to the Data Protection Board.

👥 Right to nominate

To nominate someone to exercise their rights in case of death or incapacity.

Customers have duties too: they must not give false information, impersonate someone, or file frivolous complaints. They can be fined up to ₹10,000 for doing so.
Consequences of non-compliance

What happens if we get it wrong?

Penalties under the Schedule of the Act, per instance. Tap a row for details.

Failing to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a breach▼
₹250 Cr
The biggest penalty. Weak passwords, unencrypted devices, open shared drives or no access control can all lead here after a breach.
Failing to notify the Board & affected people of a breach▼
₹200 Cr
Every breach must be reported to the Board and each affected person without delay, with a detailed report within 72 hours. This is why you must report incidents internally at once.
Violating obligations for children's data▼
₹200 Cr
Processing data of anyone under 18 without verifiable parental consent, or tracking or targeting ads at children.
Additional obligations of Significant Data Fiduciaries▼
₹150 Cr
For organisations the government notifies as "significant": DPO appointment, independent audits and impact assessments.
Breach of a voluntary undertaking given to the Board▼
₹50 Cr
Not honouring commitments made to the Board to settle a case.
Any other violation of the Act or Rules▼
₹50 Cr
Covers invalid consent, no proper notice, using data beyond its purpose, over-retention, ignoring rights requests, and similar failures.

Beyond the fine

🔍 Board inquiry

Investigations, document demands and hearings take management time and disrupt the business.

🚫 Blocking of services

For repeated violations, the government can direct that a platform or service be blocked to the public.

📰 Public reputation

Breaches make the news. In real estate, where buyers commit their life savings, lost trust is hard to win back.

🧾 Lost deals & partners

Banks, channel partners and corporate clients increasingly require DPDP compliance before working with you.

👤 Personal accountability

Employees who break data policies face disciplinary action, and data theft can bring IT Act and criminal liability.

⏳ Consent to be re-collected

Data gathered without valid consent may have to be deleted, wiping out a large part of the lead database.

Beyond the penalty

Why it matters even without the fines

Fines are the law's way of enforcing something more basic: personal data belongs to the person, and misuse of it causes real harm.

🛡️

It protects real people from real harm

A leaked Aadhaar, PAN or bank detail can be used for identity theft, loan fraud, SIM-swap scams and fake property deals. Leaked phone numbers lead to spam and harassment. Every record is a person and their family.

🤝

Trust is our product

A home is the biggest purchase most people make. They share income, family and financial details because they trust us. Protecting that data shows we deserve the trust.

🎯

Better data, better business

Consented, accurate, purpose-bound data means warmer leads, fewer complaints and higher conversion. Spam-calling unwilling contacts damages our brand and gets our numbers flagged.

🔐

Less data means less risk

Data we don't collect can't be leaked. Data we delete on time can't be stolen. Minimisation is a security strategy as well as a legal duty.

🌐

Defence against cyber-attacks

Real estate firms hold valuable financial and identity data, which makes them targets for phishing and ransomware. The security habits the Act requires are the same ones that keep attackers out.

💡

It's the right thing to do

Privacy is a fundamental right in India (Supreme Court, Puttaswamy, 2017). Treat customers' data the way you'd want a bank or hospital to treat yours.

Ask yourself before every action: "Would this customer be comfortable if they saw what I'm doing with their data right now?" If the answer is no, stop and check.
